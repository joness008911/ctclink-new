1. Web Scraper Bots
 • Pattern: Rapid requests to many pages in short time.
 • Clues: High request frequency, unusual user-agent strings (or fake ones), missing JavaScript execution, ignoring robots.txt.
 • Use Case: Data scraping, price monitoring, stealing content.

⸻

2. Spam Bots
 • Pattern: Form submissions with repeated links or keywords.
 • Clues: Similar content across multiple posts, disposable emails, identical IP ranges.
 • Use Case: Spamming comments, forums, blogs, email lists.

⸻

3. Click Bots
 • Pattern: Repeated clicks on ads or links.
 • Clues: High click rates from same device/IP, no meaningful browsing activity, unnatural session durations.
 • Use Case: Ad fraud, manipulating engagement.

⸻

4. Credential Stuffing Bots
 • Pattern: Multiple login attempts with different usernames/passwords.
 • Clues: Failed logins spike, fast login attempts, login from data center IPs or proxies.
 • Use Case: Brute force attacks, account takeover.

⸻

5. DDoS Bots
 • Pattern: Thousands of requests per second from distributed sources.
 • Clues: Sudden traffic spikes, abnormal request headers, repeated hits on same endpoints.
 • Use Case: Overloading servers to take them offline.

⸻

6. Good Bots (Legit)
 • Examples: Googlebot, Bingbot, social media crawlers.
 • Pattern: Follow robots.txt, consistent user-agent, reasonable request rate.
 • Clues: Usually from verifiable IP ranges owned by Google, Microsoft, etc.
 • Use Case: Indexing, previews, monitoring.

⸻

7. Residential Proxy Bots
 • Pattern: Harder to detect since they look like real users.
 • Clues: Still have unusual behavior (no mouse movement, same patterns of requests).
 • Use Case: Sneaker bots, ticket scalpers, scraping with human-like IPs.

⸻

👉 In short:
 • Bad bots → High speed, repetitive, ignore rules, suspicious IPs.
 • Good bots → Predictable, verifiable, polite crawling.
 • Advanced bots → Use residential IPs, simulate humans, harder to catch.

Here are detection strategies for advanced bots:

⸻

🔎 1. Behavioral Analysis
 • What to check:
 • Mouse movements → real users move in curved, imperfect ways. Bots often move in straight lines or jump instantly.
 • Typing speed → humans type with variable speed + mistakes; bots “paste” instantly.
 • Scroll behavior → humans scroll gradually, bots scroll instantly or not at all.

⸻

🔎 2. Browser & Device Fingerprinting
 • Collect unique device/browser fingerprints (screen size, timezone, fonts, plugins, canvas data).
 • Bots issue: They often use headless Chrome or Puppeteer, which miss certain attributes or report impossible combos (e.g., “Mac OS + Internet Explorer”).

⸻

🔎 3. Challenge–Response Tests
 • Hidden fields (honeypots): Bots fill every form field, including invisible ones.


⸻

🔎 4. Traffic & Rate Patterns
 • Look for:
 • Repeated visits from different IPs with the same fingerprint.
 • Too-perfect timing (e.g., login attempts exactly every 2 seconds).
 • Abnormal volume of requests during odd hours.

⸻

🔎 5. IP & Network Intelligence
 • Suspicious ASN ranges.
 • Residential proxies (harder but possible if traffic patterns don’t match real users).

⸻

🔎 6. Server-Side Validation
 • Don’t rely only on client-side checks. On the backend:
 • Verify headers (bots often spoof User-Agent but forget Accept-Language, Referer, etc.).
 • Detect cookie/session anomalies (real users carry cookies naturally, bots may restart sessions often).
 • Enforce rate limits per session/IP/device.

⸻
